Functional vs non-functional requirements

Lesson 0005 · System Design Fundamentals · Section 1 · ~6 min

Mission tie-in: this is hld-prep Phase 2, verbatim. Your coach checks four axes — functional requirements, scale numbers, latency/availability SLA, consistency model. This lesson is the theory behind that checklist and the vocabulary to nail it fast so you spend interview minutes on depth, not scoping.

1. The split

Source: Educative GSD Fundamentals — "Functional vs. Non-Functional Requirements".

Key asymmetry: NFRs shape the architecture more than FRs do. "Send a message" is a row in a table. "99.999% available" forces multi-region redundancy, failover, and the CAP choice from Lesson 0001.

2. Make every requirement measurable

Vague requirements are worthless. Replace adjectives with numbers:

  • ✗ "The system should be user-friendly / fast / reliable."
  • ✓ "Users can undo a message deletion within 10 seconds."
  • ✓ "Payment authorisation completes in under 1 second at p99."

3. Requirements conflict — on purpose

"Strong database consistency introduces latency, as the system must coordinate across nodes to guarantee the most recent data is served."

That's PACELC's "else" branch again. Perfect systems don't exist; the job is to choose. Two tools:

  • MoSCoW — Must / Should / Could / Won't-have. Forces explicit scope cuts.
  • Don't over-target — 99.999% instead of 99.9% is ~50x tighter and can multiply cost. Match the number to the business, not to ego.

"Checkout completes within 2 seconds" is which kind of requirement?

The capability is "user can check out" (FR). The "within 2 seconds" clause is a latency quality bound on that capability — a non-functional requirement, and it's exactly the kind that drives caching and datastore choices.

Recall (interleaved — pulls in earlier lessons)

An NFR says "reads may be up to 1 s stale, but p99 read latency must be < 20 ms." This points you toward…

The requirement explicitly trades consistency for latency: staleness is allowed, latency is tight. That's PACELC's "Else → Latency" (EL) posture from Lesson 0001. A linearizable/EC store spends coordination latency you were told not to spend.
